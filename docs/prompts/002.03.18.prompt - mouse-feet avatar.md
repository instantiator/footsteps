Prepare a plan to draw an avatar for `mouse-feet` mode, so a player using the mouse as their feet can see where they stand.

# Context

[002.03.02](<002.03.02.prompt - scene and avatar modes.md>) added the `mouse-feet` avatar mode: the mouse pointer is player 1's feet, so moving it over elements fires foot interactions, and a drag is a jump. [002.03.10](<002.03.10.prompt - new games and badges.md>) made it the fallback for every floor scene in Demo scenes (2.5D), and added "Play without the camera", so it is now how most people try those scenes without a camera.

Today nothing is drawn for the feet: the player sees only the normal pointer. The feet are also a single point, so a rule's `threshold` does nothing, and there's no body for effects that act on players (`player-fall`, `player-static`, `.player-*` targets), so they do nothing in `mouse-feet`.

# The avatar

- **Default:** a small circle centred on the mouse, drawn where the feet stand, and lying flat on the floor in a 2.5D scene, so it shrinks with distance like any floor item.
- **Configurable:** by the game, a stage, and a scene, eg. a different size, colour, image or template. A scene with no setting inherits its stage's, then the game's, as `modules.scene` and `modules.avatar` do. It only applies while the scene is in `mouse-feet` mode, including as a fallback when the camera is off or unavailable.
- **Mid-jump:** while a drag is a jump, show the avatar in the air (eg. lifted, with a shadow left on the floor), and land it where the jump lands.
- **Depth:** in a 2.5D scene it sorts among floor items by depth, as a camera player does.
- **The pointer:** consider hiding the system pointer over the scene while the avatar shows, or leaving it, as a setting.

Work out where the setting lives. It could sit alongside `avatar` (eg. `avatar.feet`), or in its own block. Keep it close to the avatar mode it belongs to.

# Things to consider

- **Feet with an area:** give the feet the avatar's size, so `threshold` means something by mouse, measured against the avatar as it does for a camera player's foot.
- **Player effects:** with something drawn, effects that act on players could act on the avatar (falling, spinning away, static, invisible), so `mouse-feet` play looks like camera play. Decide whether that belongs here or in [996 (player avatars)](<996.00.00.prompt - player avatars.md>), and say which in the plan.
- **Spectators:** if [002.03.16](<002.03.16.prompt - spectators.md>) is built first, the mouse avatar stays a player.
