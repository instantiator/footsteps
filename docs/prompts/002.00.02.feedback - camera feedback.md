## Flicker

The camera flickers on and off, depending on how well the model has identified / segmented any person in the image. To avoid that, propose a solution.

Here's an early suggestion, that may not be ideal:

> If it seems like a person has disappeared from the frame, retain the mask for up to a fixed period, controlled by a configured value in `.env`.
>
> This effectively means retaining pixels from the mask (as the mask's resolution) for the defined period. During that time:
>
> - Retain those pixels for the configured period, then
> - Fade the effect of those pixels of the mask to smooth their disappearance, over a short period
>
> eg. Hold camera pixels for 1s, and then fade away over 0.5s
>
> Restore the transparency of the mask as soon as those mask pixels become active.

The difficulty here is that when a player _moves around_ we don't want them to have a trail fading pixels following them around. Propose a solution that helps to avoid this. If it's possible to distinguish pixels from a person who has 'flickered' (ie. the model has stopped detecting them for a moment) vs. pixels from a person who has just moved away from their location, that would help. What do you recommend?

Select an approach by consultation with the user during planning, and the plan should implement the chosen approach. The plan should also document all approaches considered as an ADR.

## Jump

What constitues a jump is determined by the application, but isn't easily configurable. A jump should involve:

- a period of no (or little) upwards motion
- upward movement of the feet for a given period (for some or all of which they are within the element that has the interaction defined)
  - sideways motion is permitted alongside the upwards motion (ie. a jump to the left), but there must be _some_ upwards motion
- possibly, but not necessarily, some period where the feet are not within the element that the interaction has defined, moving in (probably) any direction (any motion allowed for that period)
- downard movement of the feet (possibly starting outside the element), ending the downward motion inside the element
  - sideways motion is permitted alongside the downards motion

Please challenge this definition of a jump! It may not be suitable for our kind of detection. At the moment it only covers jumping up and down on a single element. Is there a model that we can implement with the rules we have that covers jumping from one element to another?

Perhaps we should also define interactions on elements like:

- `jump-up`
- `jump-down`

That might allow us to detect a jump (from any element) to a specific element, regardless of where the player came from. To ensure accuracy, `jump-up` could be triggered only if the player's motion was successfully identified as a jump (ie. on completion would probably be ok).

The allowable periods for the various parts of a jump should be configurable through `.env` files, as should other thresholds - ie.

- What velocity or step counts as upwards motion?
- How long might a player spend jumping?
  - How much of that is expected to have upwards motion of the feet?
  - How much of that is expected to have downwards motion of the feet?
- What variance in motion is permitted?
  (eg. perhaps to tolerate some margins of error from the camera model detecting feet, or if the model stops detecting the person during a jump)

Prepare some recommended values, based on real jump times from non-athletic adults and children, and write them into `.env`

## Latency

The frame rate is still pretty low - coming in at 4-6 fps, while the application is running.

This could be caused by the camera model, any post-processing we do, or other activity that takes place, consuming CPU time, while the camera is running:

- tracking edges of every significant object in the display
- calculating key positions (feet, hands, head)
- feathering (if we implement it)
- any other React component rendering that's going on at the same time
- any inefficiencies or errors resulting in wasted rendering

Please review the code base - it would be good to understand how we can keep the frame rate high without compromising the quality of the body segmentation model.
