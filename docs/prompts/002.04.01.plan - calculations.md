## Context

Players can't tell whether their room is big enough to play, or where to put the camera, until they try calibrating. The
only guidance is the worked default in `docs/camera-mode.md` ("The default floor"): a 4:3 webcam with a 42° vertical FOV,
2 m high, tilted 22°, giving a floor about 2.6 m × 2 m that runs from 2.5 m to 4.5 m away. This plan adds a `/setup`
page. The user enters their space, and the page recommends the floor size, the camera's distance, height and tilt, and
the margins, with an isometric drawing that updates as values change.

### What the recommendation optimises (user)

These goals take precedence over matching the default calibration. The default floor is a sanity check (below), not
a target. Where the two disagree, the goals win, and the recommended floor may differ from the default corners.

1. The floor fits in the view, with a little space around it (`edgeInset`, as the default calibration does), because
   tracking is weak at the picture's edges.
2. The floor is as large as it can be without breaking the other rules.
3. The tallest player fits head to toe at both the front and the back, with `headroom` above their head.
4. No player is wildly large or small for the floor: the floor stays within a range of multiples of the players'
   heights.

### Decisions taken

| Decision             | Choice                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Maths (user)         | Trig only at the edges: `tan` once to turn the FOV from degrees into a slope, and `atan` once to give the tilt in degrees. Everything else uses + − × ÷, powers and square roots (the tan-addition formula on slopes, and the quadratic formula).                                                                                                                                                        |
| FOV input (user)     | Diagonal FOV (spec sheets from Logitech, Microsoft and others quote "dFoV") plus aspect ratio, defaulting to 65° and 4:3 (the same as the existing 42° vertical assumption). Fallback and check: an optional measurement, "camera X m from a wall sees Y m across". When filled in, it overrides the diagonal and shows the diagonal FOV it implies, so a user can also check a spec-sheet figure.       |
| Player size (user)   | A safeguard. The floor's width and depth are clamped to at most `maxFloorMultiple` × the shortest player's height, so no player looks tiny. If either ends up below `minFloorMultiple` × the tallest player's height (the space can't fit more), the page warns that players will be large for the floor.                                                                                                |
| Margins (user)       | Four minimum margins, each the space taken between the floor and a wall: left, right, far (beyond the floor), and behind the camera (where the player's screen stands). The floor sits between the left and right margins, with any spare width split evenly between them. The camera is on the floor's centre line.                                                                                     |
| Margin defaults      | Not zero: left, right and far default to 0.3 m, and behind the camera to 0.5 m (for the screen).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Tilt as aim point    | Next to the tilt in degrees, the page gives the **aim point**: how far along the floor the centre of the picture lands. It's easier to check with a tape measure than with a protractor.                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Edge inset           | `edgeInset` (10%) shrinks the usable slopes: `s' = s × (1 − inset)`. This is exact, because the image plane is linear in slope.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| Camera height (user) | Height is a recommendation, not an input. The user can give their ceiling height (optional) and the clearance needed above the camera (default 0.2 m). The highest the camera may go is `hCap = min(maxCameraHeight, ceiling − clearance)`, with `maxCameraHeight` 3 m. The page recommends the **lowest** height in `[minCameraHeight, hCap]` that gives the largest floor, because it's easier to rig. |
| Tilt limit (user)    | `maxTilt` (35°, adjustable; the default calibration's 22° is the sanity check) stops the angle getting extreme. It's converted to a slope once, as a constant, and keeps the camera far enough back that its tilt never exceeds it.                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Page and links       | Route `/setup` (prerendered), linked from Home and from the game landing page's camera/calibration area. No new dependencies: native `<input type="number">`, `<details>`, and an inline SVG.                                                                                                                                                                                                            |

### Calculations

Steps 2–8 are a function of the camera height, `layoutAt(h)`. Step 0 chooses `h`.

| Step | Name                        | Calculation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Notes                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ---- | --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 0    | Choose the height           | `hCap = min(maxCameraHeight, ceiling − clearance)`. Run `layoutAt(h)` for `h` from `min(hCap, max(minCameraHeight, Hmax + headroom))` to `hCap` in 1 cm steps. (Changed in stage 1: starting at `minCameraHeight` chose a 1 m camera below every head in large rooms.) Keep the largest `W·D`, and on a tie (within 1%) the lowest `h`.                    | At most 200 runs. It's a plain scan, so it doesn't rely on the area rising steadily with height, which the tilt limit breaks.                                                                                                        |
| 1    | Slopes                      | `sd = tan(diagFov/2)`; `sv = sd / √(1+aspect²)`; `sh = sv × aspect`. When measured: `sh = (width/2) / distance`, `sv = sh / aspect`. Then `sv`, `sh` ×= `1 − edgeInset`. `T = 2sv / (1 − sv²)`. `tMax = tan(maxTilt)`; `pMax = (tMax + sv) / (1 − tMax·sv)`.                                                                                               | This is the only use of `tan`. `T` is the tan of the usable vertical view. `pMax` is the steepest slope down to the near feet that the tilt limit allows. Shrinking the slopes is exact, because the image plane is linear in slope. |
| 2    | Head drop                   | `c = h − Hmax − headroom`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | The drop from the camera to just above the tallest head. It's negative when the camera is lower.                                                                                                                                     |
| 3    | Caps                        | `Dcap = min(maxFloorDepth, maxFloorMultiple × Hmin)`; `Wcap = min(Wr − left − right, maxFloorWidth, maxFloorMultiple × Hmin)`; `Lb = L − behind − far`                                                                                                                                                                                                     | `Lb` is the length left for the camera's distance plus the floor.                                                                                                                                                                    |
| 4    | Closest the camera can be   | `Db = c ≥ 0 ? Dcap : 0`. `dMin` is the positive root of `T·d² + (T·Db − (h − c))·d + h·(T·c − Db) = 0`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Goals 1 and 3. The distance at which the view spans the near feet to the binding head exactly. The far head binds when the camera is above head height, and the near head when it's below.                                           |
| 5    | Distance for full width     | `z(d) = √(d² + h²) / √(1 + sv²)`; `dWide = √((Wcap / 2sh)² · (1 + sv²) − h²)`, or 0 if the value under the root is negative                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Goal 2. `z` is the front edge's distance along the camera's axis, with the edge on the bottom of the view, and `2·sh·z` is the width visible there.                                                                                  |
| 6a   | Place the floor: short room | If `c ≥ 0` and `dMin + Dcap > Lb`: `dFar = Lb`; `q = c / dFar`; `p = (q + T) / (1 − q·T)`; `dNear = h / p`, or 0 if `1 − q·T ≤ 0`                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | The far edge goes to the room's limit. `q` is the slope down to the far head, and `p` the slope down to the near feet. `1 − q·T ≤ 0` means the camera sees straight down.                                                            |
| 6b   | Place the floor: otherwise  | `dNear = max(dMin, min(dWide, Lb − Dcap))`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Moves the camera back for width while the room allows.                                                                                                                                                                               |
| 6c   | Tilt limit                  | `dNear = max(dNear, h / pMax)`; `D = min(Dcap, Lb − dNear)`; `dFar = dNear + D`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Applies after 6a or 6b.                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| 7    | Tilt                        | `p = h / dNear`; `t = (p − sv) / (1 + p·sv)`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | The bottom of the usable view goes on the near feet. `t` is the tilt as a slope.                                                                                                                                                     |
| 8    | Width                       | `W = min(Wcap, 2·sh·z(dNear))`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| 9    | Warnings                    | `D ≤ 0` or `W ≤ 0`: "not enough space: raise the ceiling limit, use a wider lens, or reduce the margins". `D` or `W` below `minFloorMultiple × Hmax`: "players will be large for this floor". `hCap < minCameraHeight`: "the ceiling is too low for the camera". `h < Hmax`: "the camera is below head height, so players in front may hide those behind". |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |

### Stage 1: The calculations (Opus)

**Brief:**

- `src/components/setup/SetupCalculations.ts` holds:
  - the types `SetupInputs`, `SetupConstants` and `SetupResult`;
  - `DEFAULT_SETUP_CONSTANTS`;
  - one pure function, `planSetup(inputs, constants): SetupResult`, following steps 0–9 in order, with at most one
    short helper per step (for example, `positiveRoot(a, b, c)`).
- `SetupResult` holds every output above, plus `intermediate: { sv, sh, T, h, c, dMin, dWide, p, t }` for the
  explanation, and `warnings: string[]`.
- Metres and degrees throughout. TSDoc on each exported type and constant. No React and no DOM.

**Tests** (`tests/SetupCalculations.test.ts`). Each one re-derives the angles with `atan` in the test, rather than
trusting the intermediates:

- The default webcam slopes match a 42° vertical FOV within 1°.
- A large room, with the camera above head height:
  - the near feet sit on the bottom of the usable view, and the far head plus headroom is on or inside its top;
  - `D = Dcap`;
  - the camera is moved back until `W = Wcap`.
- A short room: `dFar = Lb`, the far head is exactly on the top of the view, and `D < Dcap`.
- A narrow room: `W = Wr − left − right`. With unequal margins, the floor is centred in the space between them.
- A camera below head height: the near head plus headroom is on the top of the view, and the far heads are inside it.
- `maxFloorMultiple` caps the floor for a short player; `minFloorMultiple` warns for a tall player in a small room.
- A measured FOV overrides the diagonal and reports the diagonal it implies.
- Height choice:
  - in a large room, it picks the lowest height that reaches the capped floor (below `hCap`);
  - in a short room, it goes higher to fit more floor;
  - a ceiling lowers `hCap` (`ceiling − clearance`), and a blank ceiling uses `maxCameraHeight`;
  - the tilt never exceeds `maxTilt`.
- The no-space warning; `1 − q·T ≤ 0` gives `dNear = 0` without NaN.
- The docs' example (`h = 2`, 42° vertical, inset 0, large room): a tilt of 22° ± 1° and a near distance of 2.1 m ± 0.1 m.

**Gate:** `dev-qual/scripts/check.sh --fast`; `npx vitest run tests/SetupCalculations.test.ts`.

### Stage 3: The isometric illustration (Opus; done directly, as the context was already loaded)

**Brief:**

- `src/components/setup/SetupIllustration.tsx` is an inline SVG, drawn from a `SetupResult` and the inputs. It uses an
  isometric projection with fixed constants (`sx = (x − y)·√3/2`, `sy = (x + y)/2 − z`), scaled to fit a `viewBox`.
- What it draws:
  - the room's outline;
  - the game floor, filled;
  - the screen and camera at the back wall's margin, with the camera at height `h`;
  - the view's top and bottom rays;
  - the tallest player as a simple upright figure at the front and at the back of the floor;
  - dimension lines with labels for `W`, `D`, `dNear`, `h`, the tilt, and all four margins.
- It has `role="img"` and an `aria-label` summarising the numbers. Its colours come from CSS variables in
  `SetupPlanner.css`, for light and dark modes. It replaces stage 2's placeholder.

**Tests** (`tests/SetupIllustration.test.tsx`):

- each labelled dimension shows its value;
- the floor polygon's points change when `W` changes;
- with no space (`D ≤ 0`), it shows a message instead of a degenerate shape.
