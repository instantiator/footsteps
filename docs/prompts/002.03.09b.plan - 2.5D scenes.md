## Context

A 2.5D scene's whole viewport takes the floor's shape, about 1.24:1 with the default floor, and sits centred on the page. On any other monitor shape, the rest of the screen is blank. The feedback asks for:

- Scenes that fill any width of screen.
- A simpler, more intuitive model of the surfaces around the floor, as in the illustration.
- The existing games updated so they leave no blank space.
- The scene tutorials prompt extended with a scene-creation toolkit.

### Decisions taken

| Decision    | Choice                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Approach    | **Screen and scene box.** In 2.5D, the viewport fills the screen. Each template's body becomes a **scene box** at the floor's shape, centred and standing on the screen's bottom edge. Authors' HTML, `%` and `cqw`/`cqh` mean what they mean now. Surfaces, players and `backgroundStyle` reach the whole screen.                                                                                                                                            |
| Naming      | **The prompt's model.** `floor-background` becomes the endless plane: it absorbs `ground-background`, and gains `data-floor-depth` to set it below the floor. The floor itself is `data-floor-area="0 0 100 100"`. `horizon-background` is a far rear plane that always fills, and is where the floor background stops. That replaces the fixed `GROUND_CUTOFF` and closes its outstanding issue. `ground-background` goes.                                   |
| Wall height | **Content by default.** The wall's box keeps its own shape, so its height to width sets the wall's height. `data-floor-height` fixes the height; what happens to overflow (clip or `background-repeat`) is the author's CSS. Boolean `data-fill-height` and, for the rear only, `data-fill-width` stretch to the screen's edges. `data-fill-edge="near\|far"` picks which end of a side wall reaches the top. It defaults to `far`, which never leaves a gap. |
| Scene shape | **Keep the floor's crop**, with or without a camera, so a scene looks the same in mouse and camera modes. The prompt's square assumption is challenged: it would shift scenes between modes.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Scale       | **1 floor unit = 1 `cqw` of the scene box.** Wherever the engine sizes a surface's box (the floor background, a filled wall), it uses this scale. Repeated textures then keep one size across the floor and up the walls, rather than stretching to the box. This fixes the visible tiling of the trifle's water.                                                                                                                                             |
| Screen fill | **No new property.** In 2.5D, a scene's `backgroundStyle` fills the whole screen, which covers "something other than white" when there are no surfaces.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Other modes | **2D scenes are unchanged.** Several 2.5D challenges side by side aren't supported, as now: one floor per screen.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |

### Positions, sizes and units (for reading; tutorials come in 002.03.12)

- **The screen** is the whole game area. **The scene box** is a rectangle at the floor's shape, as wide as it can be, standing on the screen's bottom edge and centred. In camera mode, it is the part of the camera image that holds the floor.
- **Ordinary HTML** in a template lays out in the scene box. `%`, `cqw` and `cqh` are fractions of it, so `50cqw` is half the scene box's width on any screen.
- **Floor coordinates** run 0–100: x from the floor's left to its right edge, y from its back to its front. They can go outside 0–100 (behind the back edge, off to the sides). Negative distances are behind or beside the floor.
- **Heights** (`data-floor-height`, wall heights, `data-floor-depth`) are in floor units: 100 is the floor's width.
- **Box scale:** a surface box 100 `cqw` wide spans the floor's full width. Textures in `cqw` keep their size on the floor.
- **Floor items** (`data-floor-y` and so on) stand at a floor spot and scale with distance. **Surfaces** (the classes below) are drawn in the floor's perspective. Everything else is plain 2D in the scene box.

### Scene elements, after this plan

Stage 5 moves every existing element in both games (Nanny's Magic Trifle and the demo) onto this model. That covers templates, CSS and `game-repository.json`. Afterwards, no game uses `ground-background`, the old `floor-background` meaning, or relies on the old fixed box size, and every game validates with no warnings.

| Class or attribute                                       | Applies to                  | Meaning                                                                                                        |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `data-floor-y`, `data-floor-x`                           | any element                 | Stands the element on that floor spot (x defaults to where the layout put it).                                 |
| `data-floor-height`                                      | floor item                  | Its height in floor units; it scales with distance.                                                            |
| `data-floor-anchor`                                      | floor item                  | Which point of the element stands on the spot (default bottom centre).                                         |
| `data-floor-thickness`                                   | floor item                  | Depth of floor it covers, for feet interactions.                                                               |
| `data-floor-area="left back right front"`                | any element                 | Lays the element flat on that floor rectangle. Feet count only where it's drawn. `"0 0 100 100"` is the floor. |
| `floor-background`                                       | class                       | The endless floor plane, across the screen, from the horizon to the screen's bottom.                           |
| `data-floor-depth`                                       | `floor-background`          | How far below the floor the plane lies (default 0), such as water below a bank.                                |
| `rear-wall`, `left-wall`, `right-wall`, `front-wall`     | class                       | Upright surfaces on the floor's edges.                                                                         |
| `rear-background`, `left-background`, `right-background` | class                       | Upright surfaces further out, behind the walls (default distance 50).                                          |
| `horizon-background`                                     | class                       | The furthest rear plane, always filling the screen behind everything; the floor background stops at it.        |
| `data-wall-distance`                                     | walls, backgrounds, horizon | How far out from the floor's edge it stands, in floor units.                                                   |
| `data-floor-height`                                      | walls, backgrounds          | Fixes its height; otherwise its box's shape sets it.                                                           |
| `data-fill-height`                                       | walls, backgrounds          | Stretches it up to the screen's top.                                                                           |
| `data-fill-width`                                        | rear wall, rear background  | Stretches it sideways to the screen's edges.                                                                   |
| `data-fill-edge="near\|far"`                             | side walls and backgrounds  | With `data-fill-height`, which end reaches the top (default `far`, which never leaves a gap).                  |
| `noClicks`                                               | class, any wrapper          | Lets the pointer through to what's beneath; its children still take it.                                        |

### Stage 1: Scene box geometry

**Brief:** in `FloorGeometry.ts`:

- `sceneBox(screenAspect, sceneAspect): Rect` gives the scene box in screen fractions: as large as fits, bottom-aligned and centred.
- `cropPlacement(crop, mirrored, scene?)` places the camera image so the crop fills the scene box, not the whole viewport. `CropPlacement` gains `top`. `FloorView` gains `scene: Rect`.
- `floorToViewport` and `viewportToFloor` keep their signatures. They now work in screen fractions, through the placement.

**Tests** (`tests/FloorGeometry.test.ts`): on a 21:9 and a 9:16 screen, the scene box is centred and bottom-aligned; the floor's corners land in it; round trips still hold; mirroring still swaps sides. Existing callers pass with a full-screen scene box.

### Stage 2: Screen and scene box layout

**Brief:**

- `viewportShapeOf` returns `"fill"` in 2.5D. `useShownFloor` measures the viewport (a `ResizeObserver`) to build `FloorView.scene`.
- The viewport gets a `scene-2-5d` class and a `--scene-aspect` variable. CSS under it makes each `.templateBody` the scene box: `position: absolute`, `aspect-ratio`, bottom-centred, `container-type: size`. `.templateContent`, with `backgroundStyle`, still fills the screen.
- Floor items: the default x and the layout offset are measured against the scene box. In `PlayerInteractionHook`, `nearTarget`'s `sceneWidth` uses the scene box.
- The character canvas, the debug overlay (`imageBoxStyle` gains `top`) and mouse-feet follow the placement unchanged.
- Surfaces' screen coverage (`backgroundQuad`, `groundQuad`, `horizonQuad`) covers the screen rect, not the scene box.

**Tests:**

- Browser (`floor.spec.ts`): at 1600×600 and 600×900, the scene box is centred and bottom-aligned, a floor item lands on its floor spot, and a floor area is hit where it's drawn.
- The existing rules, floor and camera specs pass.

**Gate:** `check.sh --fast`, `npx vitest run`, `scripts/run-browser-tests.sh`.

### Stage 3: The surface model

**Brief** (`FloorSurfaces.ts`, `FloorSurfacePlacement.ts`, `FloorSurfaceClasses.ts`, `GameLayersLayout.css`):

- **Classes:** remove `ground-background`. `floor-background` becomes the endless plane, from the screen's bottom and sides back to the horizon's distance, or `DEFAULT_HORIZON_DISTANCE` when there's no horizon. It lies `data-floor-depth` below the floor, and its box is sized by the engine at the floor scale.
- **Horizon:** `horizon-background` is a rear plane at `data-wall-distance`, with a large default. It fills both ways, always. The floor background reads its distance from the same template.
- **Walls and backgrounds** share one rule:
  - The height is `data-floor-height` if set, else the box's height ÷ width × the wall's length.
  - `data-fill-height` raises the top to the screen's top: the far end for side walls by default, or the near end with `data-fill-edge="near"`.
  - `data-fill-width` (rear only) extends the base to the screen's sides.
  - When filling, the engine sizes the box at the floor scale.
- **Base CSS:** walls and backgrounds are `width: 100%; height: auto` by default; floor areas keep 100% × 100%.

**Tests** (`tests/FloorSurfaces.test.ts`, `tests/FloorSurfacePlacement.test.ts`):

- A wall's height follows its box's shape, and `data-floor-height` overrides it.
- `data-fill-height` reaches the screen's top at the chosen end; the default never leaves a gap.
- `data-fill-width` covers the screen's sides.
- The floor background meets the horizon with no gap, and with a depth lies below the floor.
- The floor scale sets box sizes.
- Every quad corner has `w > 0`.

**Gate:** `check.sh --fast` plus those tests.
