## Context

2.5D scenes need a floor that players can walk all over with their feet in view. The player marks the floor's four corners in the camera image once per camera. The engine then crops the camera to that floor and reshapes the viewport to the crop. Later prompts (.06 floor placement, .08 surfaces, .14 spectators) all convert through the floor geometry module added here.

### Derivation of the default corners

This is recorded for the user docs. The setup is a 4:3 webcam with a 42° vertical field of view, 2 m high and tilted down 22°. A row at fraction _y_ from the top looks down at `22° + atan((y − 0.5) / f)`, where `f = 0.5 / tan 21°` in image heights. Checked numerically:

| Row from top | Ground distance | Width the row covers | Floor width at 8–92% or 25–75%             |
| ------------ | --------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| 90% (front)  | 2.5 m           | 3.1 m                | 84% → 2.6 m                                |
| 55% (back)   | 4.5 m           | 5.0 m                | 50% → 2.5 m                                |
| 33%          | 7.6 m           | 8.0 m                | a 2.6 m floor is only a third of the width |

So the defaults describe a rectangle about 2.6 m wide and 2 m deep, 2.5–4.5 m from the camera. The front inset keeps feet away from the image edge.

### Stage 1: Floor geometry module

**Brief:** add `src/components/camera/FloorGeometry.ts`. All functions are pure, with a TSDoc comment each.

- `type Corners = { backLeft; backRight; frontRight; frontLeft }`, each a `Point` in image fractions. `DEFAULT_CORNERS` comes from the prompt's table.
- `floorHomography(corners)` returns `{ toImage(floor), toFloor(image) }`, with floor coordinates 0–100. Use the closed-form square→quad map and its adjugate inverse.
- `isValidFloor(corners)`: strictly convex, and wound the same way as `DEFAULT_CORNERS`.
- `dragCorner(corners, name, target)`: returns the corners with `name` at `target` if that is valid. Otherwise the corner goes to the furthest valid point along the segment, by bisection. Clamp `target` to 0–1 first.
- `floorCrop(corners)` returns `{ left, right, bottom }` as image fractions: the bounding box's sides, and its lowest point. The top is always 0.
- `cropAspect(crop, imageAspect)` returns `((right − left) × imageAspect) / bottom`.
- `isOnFloor(corners, imagePoint)` returns `toFloor` within 0–100 on both axes.
- Move `maskRectForElement`, `screenPointForMaskPoint` and `maskPointForScreenPoint` in unchanged from `CameraSupport.ts`, along with their tests, and fix the imports.

**Tests:** `tests/FloorGeometry.test.ts`

- Corners map to floor (0,0), (100,0), (100,100) and (0,100), and back again.
- A round trip of interior points works for:
  - the defaults;
  - a non-symmetrical quad;
  - a minimal sliver quad.
- Rows bunch towards the back: floor y 50 sits above the image midpoint between the edges.
- `isValidFloor` rejects:
  - a concave quad;
  - a self-crossing quad;
  - a quad with front and back swapped.
- `dragCorner`:
  - a drag past the limit stops on the boundary and stays valid;
  - a valid drag lands exactly.
- `floorCrop` uses the lowest corner on a slanted front edge.
- `cropAspect` with the defaults on 4:3 gives about 1.24.
- `isOnFloor` is true inside and false outside.

**Gate:** `dev-qual/scripts/check.sh --fast` and `npx vitest run` pass.

### Stage 3: Fitting the view to the floor

**Brief:**

- `Game.tsx` and `GameLayersLayout.tsx`:
  - Resolve the scene mode.
  - Read the corners once at start with `calibrationOrDefault(cameraKeyOf(loadCameraChoice()))`, like `mirror` at `:290`.
  - The camera's report of its ratio (`:288`) also gives the image aspect. Without a camera, assume 4/3.
- The target ratio is:
  - `2.5D`: `cropAspect(floorCrop(corners), imageAspect)`, camera or not;
  - `2D` with the camera on: the camera's ratio;
  - otherwise, the container's ratio.
    Pass the target ratio into `useSceneTransition` in place of the camera-on test. `runTransition` reshapes whenever the target ratio changes, and fades otherwise. Keep its "wait for the camera's ratio" step.
- **Crop.** In 2.5D, style `.characterCanvasStack`, not the reference canvas, so the player wrappers follow:
  - `width: calc(100cqw / (right − left))`;
  - `left: calc(−100cqw × screenLeft / (right − left))`;
  - `top: 0`;
  - height from the aspect ratio.
    Here `screenLeft = mirror ? 1 − right : left`, because `scaleX(-1)` flips around the canvas's own centre. `.gameViewport` gets `overflow: hidden`. Put the calc in one small helper in `FloorGeometry.ts` (`cropPlacement(crop, mirror)`, returning `{ widthScale, leftOffset }`), so no layout file does geometry.
- 2D scenes are unchanged.
- Rules demo: in `public/game-repository.json`, the `depth` stage's scene gets `"scene": "2.5D"`.
- Camera in dialogs: no repository game has a camera dialog scene. Add one browser check that a 2.5D template scene's `click` and `foot-enter` targets still fire after cropping. Record that dialogs are covered by the same `maskRectForElement` path.

**Tests:**

- `GameLayersLayout.test.tsx`:
  - a 2.5D scene sets the viewport ratio to the crop's;
  - a 2D → 2.5D change starts a reshape, not a fade;
  - 2.5D without a camera uses the default crop ratio;
  - mirroring flips the offset.
- `useSceneTransition` test: a ratio change with the camera on throughout still reshapes.
- `tests/browser/viewport.spec.ts`: on the rules-demo `depth` stage, with the fake camera and `seedCalibration`, the viewport's aspect matches `cropAspect` within 1%, and the canvas stack is wider than the viewport.

**Gate:** `check.sh --fast`, `npx vitest run`, and `scripts/run-browser-tests.sh viewport camera rules`.

    - "Save" saves, then shows "Saved";

### Stage 6: Cropping what the model sees (conditional)

**Brief:**

- In `CharacterCanvas.tsx` `detectFrame` (`:113-127`), when a crop is given, take the source rect from the crop plus a margin (`visionCropMargin`, an app setting in `AppConfig.ts`, default 0.1 of the image width, using the same config pattern as `visionInputWidth`). Use `createImageBitmap(video, sx, sy, sw, sh, { resizeWidth })`.
- Map the results back to full-frame mask coordinates:
  - offset the key points;
  - paste each mask into a full-size `Uint8Array` at the offset.
    `ponytail:` the paste costs one copy per person. Keep masks crop-sized end to end if profiling shows that copy matters.
- Run `VISION_BENCH=1 scripts/run-browser-tests.sh vision-bench` with and without the crop (`VISION_BENCH_QUERY`). Compare detection counts on the back-of-room photos, and the time per frame.
- **Keep it** only if people at the back are found more often, or frames are faster with no loss. Otherwise revert the stage, and record the numbers in ADR 0005 and in `docs/outstanding-issues.md`.

**Tests:** a unit test of the mask and key-point offset helper (a point at the crop origin maps to the margin's offset).

**Gate:** `check.sh --fast`, `npx vitest run`, the bench numbers recorded in ADR 0005, and `scripts/run-browser-tests.sh camera`.
