## Context

Two problems came out of playing the demo with a real camera:

1. **Players turn into spectators.** The player is often drawn transparent, as a spectator, and can't interact. Small,
   flickering patches of camera pixels appear separate from the player's silhouette.
2. **Players are giants.** Scenes feel like a model village: the rings float at shin height, the bridge is a plank, and
   the pits are missing floorboards.

### What the investigation found

**Detection.** Two settings stretch one stray blob into seconds of spectator time:

- **No blob clean-up.** BodyPix (`segmentMultiPersonParts`, threshold 0.65, refine 3; `BodyPixModel.ts:131-137`)
  assigns every foreground pixel to its nearest decoded pose, with no distance cap. Nothing removes stray patches: no
  connected components, no morphology (`BodyPixModel.ts:48-102`).
- **The floor position comes from the mask's lowest pixels.** `groundPointOf` (`PlayerPosition.ts:46-72`) takes the
  lowest row below which more than 1% of the mask's pixels lie. `PositionFollower` keeps the lowest point of the last
  700 ms (`:91-102`), so a one-frame low blob counts for 700 ms.
- **The return to playing needs an unbroken second on the floor.** `SpectatorWatch` (`Spectators.ts:56-98`) switches
  only after 1000 ms of agreeing frames beyond a 5-unit margin. A flicker resets the timer, so a blob that flickers at
  least once a second keeps a real player a spectator. Spectators get no monitors (`CharacterCanvas.tsx:395`).
- **The debug view can't show it.** Spectators have no markers, and nothing shows their off-floor distance.
- **Fixtures are still photos only.** No recordings exist.

**Scale.** Every size in the engine is relative; nothing is real-world:

- **The scene's units are relative.** Floor units are percentages of the floor's width (`FloorGeometry.ts:1-13`). The
  player is the camera frame, enlarged so the calibrated floor fills the scene box (`cropPlacement`). Nothing in the
  engine knows metres.
- **The default floor is small.** It is about 2.6 m wide, so 1 floor unit ≈ 2.6 cm and a 1.7 m player is about
  65 units tall.
- **The demo's elements are sized for a much smaller person.** Rings sit at `data-floor-height="7"`, about 18 cm. Doors
  are 18–24 units, about 46–63 cm. The pits are about 62 × 52 cm, and the bridge is about 50–60 cm wide.

## Detection options

### D1: debug view for spectators and ground points

**What's involved:**

- In `?debug=true`, draw every person, spectators included, labelled with the player number or "spectator", their
  off-floor distance, and the spectator timer.
- Mark the ground point, raw and after the window.
- After D2, tint removed blobs differently.
- Feed spectators to `FloorOverlay` (`GameLayerWrappers.tsx:128-167`).

**Pros:** cheap, and makes the camera session diagnostic.

**Cons:** none.

**Recommendation:** select, and do it first.

**Decision:** agreed.

### D2: remove stray blobs from each person's mask

**What's involved:**

- After `detect` (in `personFromParts`, `BodyPixModel.ts`, or straight after), label each person's mask by connected
  component.
- Keep the largest component, plus any component that is at least `visionBlobMinShare` of the largest (eg. 5%) and
  within `visionBlobMaxGap` of it (eg. 10% of the frame).
- Recompute the key points, head extent and scale from the kept pixels.
- Add both settings to app config, so they can be tuned by URL query.

**Pros:**

- Every consumer gets a clean mask: tracking, ground, `body-enter`, near and drawing.
- Linear-time union-find, a few milliseconds per frame. Measured with `vision-bench.spec.ts`.

**Cons:**

- Could drop a real but disconnected limb. The two settings guard against that.

**Recommendation:** select.

**Decision:** agreed.

### D3: ground point from the main body, with a gentler window

**What's involved:**

- Take the ground from the kept components.
- Replace "lowest of the last 700 ms" (`PositionFollower`) with a percentile, eg. the 80th, of the window's ground y.
- Optionally, use the feet's y when both feet are placed near the mask's bottom. The mask stays the fallback, as feet
  are often missed from behind.

**Pros:** removes the amplifier that turns a blink into most of a second off the floor.

**Cons:** slightly more lag for real steps towards the camera.

**Recommendation:** select.

**Decision:** agreed.

### D4: asymmetric, flicker-tolerant spectator hysteresis

**What's involved:**

- In `SpectatorWatch`, judge "off" or "on" by the share of frames over the hold window (80%), rather than requiring
  every frame to agree.
- Leaving the floor takes 1000 ms; returning takes 300 ms.
- New config keys go alongside `spectatorHoldMs` and `spectatorMargin`.

**Pros:** a flicker no longer pins a player as a spectator.

**Cons:** slightly more toggling at the floor's edge.

**Recommendation:** select.

**Decision:** agreed, with these values, re-tuned on camera if needed.

### D5: tune BodyPix thresholds

**What's involved:**

- After D2, sweep these settings with `vision-bench.spec.ts` on the people photos and the stray-patch fixtures:
  - `bodypixThreshold` (0.6–0.75);
  - `scoreThreshold` and `nmsRadius`, passed explicitly;
  - `bodypixRefineSteps`.
- Record the people, hands and feet found, and the frame time, for each.
- Set the best defaults, and record the sweep in the plan's outcome.
- Add the same sweep, run against real recordings, to `993.00.00.prompt - jump detection review.md` as further tuning
  work.

**Pros:** cheap, and evidence-based.

**Cons:** stills don't show flicker, so real footage (993) may move the values again.

**Recommendation:** run as a measured experiment.

**Decision:** agreed: tune it here, and add the steps to 993.

### D6: temporal mask filter

**What's involved:** keep a pixel only if it was foreground in, say, 2 of the last 3 frames.

**Pros:** removes one-frame sparkle.

**Cons:**

- A frame of lag on every limb.
- It fights gap filling.
- If the body is found unreliably, it makes recovering it less likely.

**Recommendation:** rule out.

**Decision:** ruled out for now.

### D7: player-aware exclusion zones (prompt 990.00.00)

**What's involved:**

- On the calibration page, the user marks rectangles over things that look like people but aren't: the TV, mirrors,
  posters.
- **Where pixels are removed matters, because players stand in front of these zones.** Removing pixels from the model's
  input, or from every mask, would cut a player standing in front of the TV.
- **Instead, apply it per person, after tracking and the spectator decision:**
  - A person whose ground point is on the floor (a player) keeps all their pixels, including inside zones.
  - A person whose mask lies mostly inside a zone, with no on-floor ground point, is dropped entirely, as an image,
    not a person.
  - Any person's pixels inside a zone that aren't connected to their main body (D2's components) are dropped.
- That keeps a player in front of the TV whole, while the TV's own "people" and stray blobs vanish.

**Notes:**

- The TV is usually above and behind the floor, so its "people" are already spectators, as the user notes. Zones
  matter for what they still draw and cost, and for mirrors or posters near the floor's edge.
- A mirror reflecting the player beside the floor is the hard case. Their reflection's ground point is off the floor,
  so it is dropped, which is the intended result.

**Pros:** tidies visuals beyond this problem.

**Cons:**

- New UI and per-camera storage.
- Mistakes are worse than doing nothing if they cut a player.

**Recommendation:** write it up as `docs/prompts/990.00.00.prompt - exclusion zones.md`, with the design above. Build
it in this plan only if the first camera session shows zone-shaped artefacts are a key factor in the spectator
problem.

**Decision:** prompt 990, with evidence overriding.

### D8: test fixtures

**What's involved:**

- **Unit tests** with synthetic masks:
  - a body plus a far blob: the blob is removed and the ground is unchanged;
  - a hand split by a thin gap: kept;
  - a blob that flickers every frame: the player stays a player;
  - a single low frame: the ground isn't held for 700 ms.
- **Browser tests:** the fake camera paints a person photo plus small bright patches (`fakeCamera.ts` already draws
  shapes). Positions and spectator state stay steady.
- Real recordings come later, with 993's recorder.

**Pros:** regression-proof without a camera.

**Cons:** still needs the camera session.

**Recommendation:** select.

**Decision:** agreed.

## Scale options

### S1: explainer and a redesigned `/setup` page

**Explainer.** A `<details>`, "Getting the most from your space", in plain language:

1. **Choose your playing area first,** as large as you can. A bigger area makes players smaller compared with the game,
   so scenes feel bigger.
2. **Keep it a rectangle,** and mark its four corners on the floor.
3. **Then place the camera.**
   - **Height:** a higher camera sees more floor and looks down, so feet are easier to place, but people look shorter.
   - **Distance:** further back fits a wider area, but each person is smaller and less detailed in the picture.
4. **A worked example** with the planner's numbers.

**Redesigned layout.** It matches the landing and calibration pages: panels, a header, and no long scroll of fields.

```text
┌ 👣 Footsteps — Plan your space ──────────────────────────────┐   title panel
├──────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┤
│ ▾ Your space (open)          │  Results (floor, camera       │
│   room width/depth, webcam,  │  distance, height, tilt,      │
│   tallest/shortest player    │  margins, warnings)           │
│ ▸ Space around the floor     │                               │
│ ▸ Camera details / FOV check │  Illustration                 │
│ ▸ Assumptions (Reset)        │  Screen view                  │
├──────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┤
│ ▸ Getting the most from your space   ▸ How it works          │   explainers
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- Every group of inputs is a collapsible `<details>`.
- The key inputs ("Your space") start open, and the rest start closed.
- Results stay visible beside the inputs.
- At phone width, the panels stack.
- The page reuses the landing page's panel styles.

**Pros:** asked for. It makes the least consistent page consistent, and puts the explainer where people plan.

**Cons:** the browser and unit tests of `/setup` need their locators updated.

**Recommendation:** select.

**Decision:** agreed, with the redesign. Which inputs count as key is still open (question 1).

### S2: person-scale sizes, a design guide, and a door pattern

**What's involved:**

- **Design guide** in `docs/positions-sizes-units.md`: author sizes relative to a person, using S3's values. A person
  is about 1.7 m, a waist about 0.9 m, a chest about 1.3 m, and a door about 2 m × 0.9 m.
- **Rings:** at waist-to-chest height, collected by standing near (the shadow floor area) or by a hand.
- **Doors:** person height and width, with one shared trigger pattern. Today the pits door fires on `foot-enter #door-base`,
  and the bridge door on `foot-enter #door` with `holdMs`, or `click`. A door should open on any of:
  - standing in a floor area in front of the door;
  - standing in a floor area behind it;
  - a hand on the door while the player stands near it (to either side): a `near-enter` rule sets a `nearDoor` flag,
    and `hand-enter` on the door then fires if `state.nearDoor`;
  - feet or hands touching the door itself (`foot-enter`/`hand-enter`);
  - a mouse click.

  This is written once as a documented pattern, and used for the pits and bridge doors.

- **Pit signs** at waist height. **The effects stage's floating band** at a reachable hand height.

**Pros:** direct, and works in every room once S3 exists.

**Cons:** more rules per door, and the browser tests' coordinates change.

**Recommendation:** select.

**Decision:** agreed: rings at waist to chest, reached by standing near or by hand.

### S3: floor size in metres, entered at calibration

**What's involved:**

- **On the calibration page:**
  - fields for the floor's real width and depth in metres, stored with each calibration (`FloorCalibration.ts`), and
    blank by default;
  - a clear note that the playing area must be a rectangle, with its four corners marked on the floor, as the
    corners can be dragged into any shape.
- **The engine** turns those metres into person-scale values in floor units, given to templates as Handlebars values,
  eg. `{{scale.person}}`, `{{scale.waist}}`, `{{scale.chest}}`, `{{scale.door}}`. `data-floor-height="{{scale.chest}}"`
  then works with no expression language.
- Without metres, the engine assumes the default floor (2.6 m × 2 m).
- Calibration is preferred over `/setup`, as the planner explores many arrangements, not the real one.

**Pros:** elements are life-size in any room.

**Cons:** another input that can be wrong. The note and defaults soften that.

**Recommendation:** select.

**Decision:** agreed: entered at calibration.

### S4: draw players smaller than life (spike)

**What's involved:**

- A `playerScale` (eg. 0.6) draws each player's cut-out shrunk about their ground point.
- Every hit-test uses the same transform: `maskRectForElement`, `screenPointForMaskPoint`, `bodyPartsIn`, and the
  debug overlay.
- If it's kept, it becomes an optional calibration control: a virtual player outline next to the floor, scaled up or
  down, so the user sees the effect. The user has doubts it would be intuitive for most players, so it stays optional.

**Pros:** the set feels bigger, in every game.

**Cons:**

- On its own, it shrinks the player without enlarging the world they walk.
- It touches every hit-test.

**Recommendation:** spike on one stage.

**Decision:** spike here.

### S5: amplified movement (spike, with S4)

**What's involved:**

- A motion gain `g` counts a player's position from the floor's centre at `g` × their real offset.
- Their cut-out is drawn translated to that position, and scaled by S4, so the picture and the hit-tests agree.

**Pros:** small players in a big set, walking a big floor in a small room.

**Cons:**

- Amplifies jitter, so it depends on D2–D4.
- Hands move by the real amount while the body moves by `g`. The spike should show whether that feels odd.
- With several players, on-screen collisions don't match the room.

**Recommendation:** spike with S4.

**Decision:** spike here.

### S6: conveyor belts and teleporters

**What's involved:** elements that carry or jump a player's virtual position between areas.

**Pros:** fun, and they extend a small room.

**Cons:** they change each scene's mechanic, and need S5 underneath.

**Recommendation:** rule out.

**Decision:** ruled out. Note it in 990's or a later prompt's future ideas, once S5's spike shows whether position
offsets feel right.

### S7: redesign the bridge and pits for person scale

**What's involved:**

- **Bridge:** person-width sections, longer, with more turns across the full depth, so the lava stays the bigger area
  and the peril comes from length and turns. Section 4's static reveal stays.
- **Pits:** fewer, larger pits sized to a stride, with a path needing one or two clear steps around them.

**Pros:** keeps the peril at person scale.

**Cons:** redesign effort, and test coordinates change.

**Recommendation:** select with S2.

**Decision:** agreed.

## Camera sessions (interactive pauses)

At stages 6 and 11 the work stops, and I guide a session on the user's real camera. I give one step at a time; the
user does it and reports back (what they saw, plus a screenshot where asked); I change config values (most can be
changed live by URL query) or code; and we repeat until the step passes. Findings go into the plan's outcome and into
`docs/outstanding-issues.md`.

**Session 1 (stage 6): detection.**

1. **Set up.** Pull the branch, `npm run dev`, then open `/game/rules-demo`. Choose the camera in its camera panel
   (the calibration page uses that choice), press Calibrate, and calibrate a rectangular floor. Then open Demo scenes
   with `?debug=true`.
2. **Stand still** in the middle of the floor for 10 s. Report whether your label stays "player 1" and whether you
   ever go transparent. Screenshot the debug view, showing your mask tint and any tinted removed blobs.
3. **Walk slowly to each corner,** pausing 3 s at each. Report the off-floor distance shown at each corner and any
   switch to spectator.
4. **Walk front to back and back to front,** slowly then briskly. Report any spectator flips, and where.
5. **Stand in front of the TV,** or the back wall, with arms out. Check that your whole body is drawn and none of it is
   tinted as removed.
6. **Wave both hands** away from your body. Check the hands aren't removed as blobs. If they are, I relax
   `visionBlobMaxGap`.
7. **Play Avoid the pits and Bridge across lava** once each. Report any time you couldn't interact while on the floor.
8. **Exclusion-zone evidence:** if any "person" or blob keeps appearing over the TV, a mirror or a poster, note where.
   If it's a key factor, D7 is built before the scale stages.

I then tune `visionBlobMinShare`, `visionBlobMaxGap`, the D4 holds and the D5 thresholds by URL query until steps 2–7
pass, and commit the values.

**Session 2 (stage 11): scale and the spike.**

1. **Calibrate again,** entering the floor's real width and depth.
2. **Rings:** they should float at waist-to-chest height. Collect five by hand and five by standing near. Report any
   that are hard to reach.
3. **Doors:** they should look person-sized. Open each door four ways: standing in front, standing behind, a hand on
   it from beside, and touching it. Report which work.
4. **Bridge and pits:** report whether they feel perilous at life size.
5. **Spike:** try `?playerScale=0.6` and `?motionGain=1.5` on one stage. Report how it feels, especially hands versus
   body. We decide whether to keep S4/S5, and whether to add the calibration control.

## Outcomes

### Stage 2 (D2)

- **Implementation:** `keepMainBody` (`src/vision/MaskComponents.ts`) runs on BodyPix's part map before the person is
  built. It uses 4-connected union-find, keeps the largest patch, and keeps any other patch at least
  `visionBlobMinShare` of it and within `visionBlobMaxGap` of the frame's width (box to box). Removed pixels travel as
  `strays`, which the debug tint paints magenta.
- **Cost:** none measurable. On the vision bench's photos, inference time was within noise of a run that keeps every
  patch.
- **Defaults** (bench against "keep everything", hands and feet lost):
  - **`minShare` 0.05:** lost limbs on 7 photos, including the single-person `active-skateboard-trick` (2 hands) and
    `active-sprint-start` (1 hand), as feet and outstretched hands can be separate small patches.
  - **0.01:** lost limbs on 5 photos.
  - **0.002:** lost limbs only on 3 crowded group photos, where a "limb" is likely someone else's pixels.
  - **`maxGap` 0.05 rather than 0.1:** no better.
  - **Chosen:** `visionBlobMinShare` 0.002 and `visionBlobMaxGap` 0.1. The distance does most of the work: stray
    patches far from the body go, whatever their size.

### Stage 4 (D8, D5)

- **Stray-patch fixtures through the real model aren't deterministic.** A fake-camera option painting small copies of a
  person photo low in the corners produced no strays: BodyPix didn't segment them, and the player stayed a steady
  player with or without stray removal. Stray removal is covered by `tests/MaskComponents.test.ts`, the ground
  percentile by `tests/PlayerPosition.test.ts`, and flicker by `tests/Spectators.test.ts`. The probe and the option
  were removed. Real stray patches wait for 993's recordings.
- **D5 sweep** on the vision bench's 26 photos. Error is the sum of the differences from the expected people, hands
  and feet.

  | Setting                                     | Total error | Mean inference |
  | ------------------------------------------- | ----------- | -------------- |
  | `bodypixThreshold` 0.65, refine 3 (current) | 120         | 107 ms         |
  | `bodypixThreshold` 0.6                      | 118         | 111 ms         |
  | `bodypixThreshold` 0.7                      | 125         | 104 ms         |
  | `bodypixThreshold` 0.75                     | 128         | 102 ms         |
  | `bodypixRefineSteps` 5                      | 123         | 122 ms         |

- **No defaults changed.** The differences are small. 0.6 is marginally better on stills, but a lower threshold admits
  more stray pixels, which is the problem being fixed, and stills can't show flicker. `scoreThreshold` and `nmsRadius`
  aren't settings yet. They are left to 993's sweep on real recordings, with the camera session's evidence.

### Stage 7 (S3)

- **Storage:** the floor size is saved under its own key, `footsteps:floor-sizes`, by the same camera key as the
  corners, so saved corners keep their shape. It is saved and cleared with them.
- **Calibration page:** step 1 now asks for a rectangle, and a row under the picture holds the optional width and depth
  in metres with the rectangle note. A first try put it in the left column, which then scrolled at 1280×800.
- **Template values:** `scale.metre`, `metreDeep`, `person`, `waist`, `chest`, `door` and `doorWidth`, in floor units, to
  one decimal place. `metreDeep` is there because floor y runs 0–100 front to back, whatever the depth.

### Stage 10 (S4 + S5 spike)

- **How it works:** `VirtualPlayers.ts` gives each player a placement: shrunk by `playerScale` about their ground point,
  which moves to their position scaled out from the floor's centre by `motionGain`, and resized for the floor's width
  at the new depth. The drawn canvas moves by CSS, and the masks and key points the monitors read are resampled the
  same way, so hit-tests match the picture. Spectators and the debug list still use the real position.
- **Checked** in a browser with a photo: at `playerScale=0.6&motionGain=1.5` the cut-out is smaller and further out, and
  the head, hand and feet markers sit on it.
- **Off by default,** and not yet judged on a real camera: camera session 2 decides whether to keep it, and whether to
  add the calibration control.
