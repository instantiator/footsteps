Track the intention to add player avatars. This prompt is a placeholder: it isn't ready to plan yet.

# Context

[002.03.02](<002.03.02.prompt - scene and avatar modes.md>) adds an avatar mode to game data, saying what stands in for the player, with three modes: `mouse`, `camera` and `mouse-feet`. This prompt adds avatars that are drawn rather than cut out of the camera image.

# Intended avatar modes

| Mode               | Meaning                                                                                                             |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `2D-mouse-avatar`  | Like a point-and-click adventure: click a place and the avatar travels there. Drawn in 2D.                          |
| `3D-mouse-avatar`  | As `2D-mouse-avatar`, drawn in 3D.                                                                                  |
| `2D-camera-avatar` | Drawn instead of the camera cut-out. The player's head, hands and feet positions pose the avatar's skeleton. In 2D. |
| `3D-camera-avatar` | As `2D-camera-avatar`, drawn in 3D.                                                                                 |

"2D" and "3D" describe how the avatar is drawn, not where it can be used. Every avatar should work in every scene mode (2D, 2.5D, and 3D from [997.00.00](<997.00.00.prompt - 3D scenes.md>)), using that scene's coordinate system.

# Notes

- Mouse avatars build on `mouse-feet` from 002.03.02: the avatar's position is the feet position, and it walks towards each click rather than jumping to it.
- **Camera avatars suit a pose model.** They need joints (shoulders, elbows, hips, knees), but the app's `KeyPoints` only carry hands, feet and head. BodyPix works these out from the extents of its body-part segments, so it has no real joints. MoveNet MultiPose, already implemented, finds 17 joints for up to 6 people, placed hands and feet as well as BodyPix in the [ADR 0005](../ADRs/0005-vision-models.md) bench, and ran at about 28 fps against BodyPix's 10. Its weakness was its cut-outs, which an avatar doesn't need, so camera avatar scenes could skip segmentation entirely. MediaPipe Pose has more points, including heels and toes, but found only one or two people in a group.
- **What to check first:** widening `KeyPoints` to carry joints; how well MoveNet places joints from behind ([ADR 0010](../ADRs/0010-player-tracking-from-behind.md)); and what happens when a game mixes cut-out and avatar scenes, as that means switching models, or running two, mid-game.
