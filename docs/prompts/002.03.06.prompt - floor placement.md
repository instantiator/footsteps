Prepare a plan to place items on the floor in 2.5D scenes, make players' positions available to variables, and convert the existing games to 2.5D.

# Context

This follows [002.03.04](<002.03.04.prompt - calibration.md>), which adds calibration, the 2.5D scene mode, and the floor geometry module. All conversions between the camera image, the viewport and the floor go through that module.

Every room's calibration differs, so authors can't hand-place items on the floor safely, and items further back should shrink with distance. Give items floor coordinates and let the engine place and scale them. Floor coordinates run 0–100 across the floor at the item's depth, and 0–100 from back to front, so every floor point is reachable in any room.

# Floor-placed items

![Floor coordinates, an item's anchor, height and thickness, and the unreachable corners](<illustrations/002.03.06 - floor coordinates.svg>)

| Attribute              | Meaning                                                                                                                              | Default                                                       |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `data-floor-y`         | How far forward the item stands: 0 at the back edge, 100 at the front. Its presence makes the item floor-placed.                     | required                                                      |
| `data-floor-x`         | Where across the floor it stands: 0 at the left edge, 100 at the right, at that depth.                                               | where the layout puts it across the viewport, read as floor x |
| `data-floor-height`    | How high above the floor its anchor sits, in the same units as x, so it shrinks with distance.                                       | `0`                                                           |
| `data-floor-anchor`    | The point on the item that sits at those coordinates, in CSS `transform-origin` syntax: `center bottom`, `left center`, `40% 70%`, … | `center bottom`, the point that touches the floor             |
| `data-floor-thickness` | The band of floor y, centred on the item, that a player must stand in for their camera interactions with it to count.                | none: interactions count from anywhere, as they do now        |

- **Placement and scale.** Authors size an item as it would appear at the front edge. The engine scales it by the floor's width at its depth relative to the front edge's, and moves its anchor to the floor point. It uses the CSS `translate` and `scale` properties, not `transform`, so effects and animations that use `transform` (like the stepping stones' `floatAndZoom`) still work, and the layout is untouched.
- **Depth.** A floor-placed item's depth is its floor y, and players' depths become their floor y too. This replaces `data-depth`, which fails validation with a message naming `data-floor-y`. 2D scenes no longer sort players by depth.
- **Height.** Only floor-placed items have a height. An item with no `data-floor-y` is laid out as it is today.
- **Layering.** Items that aren't floor-placed keep today's layers: background items behind everything, challenge foreground items in front of the players like a HUD, so dialogs, scrolls, and anything else we invent keep working. Overview and stage foregrounds are drawn behind the players, because they must stay beneath challenge backgrounds. Floor-placed items there are placed and scaled, but only challenge foreground items sort among the players. This suits flat things on the floor, like stepping stones.
- **Backgrounds.** A scene's background image set to `cover`, like Nanny's Magic Trifle's river, still fills the reshaped viewport. It doesn't matter that it doesn't line up with the floor; [002.03.08](<002.03.08.prompt - floor areas walls and backgrounds.md>) adds surfaces that do.
- **In 2D scenes**, `data-floor-*` attributes are a validation warning, as they do nothing there.

# The back of the floor

The floor narrows towards the back, so a scene's back corners might not be reachable. Floor-placed items solve this for anything that matters, as every floor point is reachable. This was chosen over sliding each player sideways by depth (people drift sideways when walking forwards, and jitter is amplified), and over warping the camera image to a rectangle (it distorts bodies, and costs time every frame).

Debug mode shades the unreachable area, and warns when an interactive item that isn't floor-placed sits inside it.

# Players' positions

In 2.5D scenes, each player's floor x and y come from their ground point through the inverse homography. In 2D scenes, their position is their ground point as a percentage of the scene. While a player is jumping, hold their position from take-off, as player depth does today. With `mouse-feet`, the mouse is player 1, and its position goes through the same mapping.

There are two consumers, with different needs:

- **The engine**, sorting players among items and testing thickness, uses each frame's position, smoothed as player depth is now. It never touches state or templates.
- **Variables**, for rules and templates (eg. `players.1.x`, `players.1.y`, `players.count`), get quantised, rate-limited positions, so templates don't re-render every frame:
  - **Quantise** to a grid, eg. 20 × 20 cells of 5 units. A position only changes when the player crosses into another cell.
  - **Hysteresis.** Only move to a new cell once the player is a quarter of a cell past its edge, so standing on a boundary doesn't flicker. This is the same idea as `cameraDepthDeadband`.
  - **Rate limit.** Publish at most, say, 10 times a second, sending the latest positions each time. Updates come from camera or mouse frames, so they can never be faster than the frame rate.
  - **Batch.** Publish every player's position in one state change, so `when` rules see a consistent picture and fire once.
  - The grid size and rate are app settings, like the camera's depth settings.
