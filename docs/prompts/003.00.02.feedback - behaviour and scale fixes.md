## Player detection

Playing through some of the games, the spectator detection seems to be a little off. The player seemed to be transparent a very high proportion of the time, and unable to interact with the game when in that state.

Speculation about possible causes:

1. Body part segmentation didn't correctly detect the feet of the player, so couldn't place them inside the floor area of the scene
2. The segmentation model associated pixels elsewhere on the screen with the player, which confused the player's position (are we using the lowest pixel of the player, or the lowest pixel segmented as foot of some sort?)
3. Something is wrong with how spectators are identified, and so the player is misidentified as a spectators, even thought they are detected inside the floor area
4. A portion of the TV that shows the game is visible in the camera's view - this might possibly also be interpreted as a player image, or part of a player image
5. Something else?

User input: Cause 2 seemed to be a contributor. The camera layer for the player seemed to include a few small patches of pixels from the camera's stream, quite separate from the player image. (These pixels also seemed a little flickery.) (It's also possible the model was detecting these blobs of pixels as separate people / body segments, and unrelated to the player. In which case this would be a red herring. Unclear at the current time.) If there's a way to remove those unwanted extra blobs of pixels, that would be a good start.

Explore the options, determine how to test them, and plan tests and fixes for these issues.

## Scaling

Scenes play out as if the player is a giant in a set the scale of a model village.

- The rings are at shin height
- The bridge over lava feels like shuffling along a small plank
- The pits feel like missing floorboards in a small room

We need ways to make better use of space.

The actual size of the playing area is one limitation, and a better understanding of how height and distance and angle of the camera can alter the playing experience would help. Write a short explainer for the user, in simple language, and place it in an expanding section on the `setup/` page. A process for getting started might be:

- Figure out the size of the playing area you'd like, or maximise the playing area for the space you have
- Then figure out how best to arrange the camera in relation to it

Explain how height of the camera affects the playing field, and how distance of the camera from the playing field affects it.

One option to improve the scaling issue might be to scale down the camera view. This may require some careful calculation. Include a short description of the issue in the plan. It may not work - as this would also reduce the area that players can walk around on screen.

Another option is to scale up the elements inside the space (eg. the doors should be approximate person height and approximately person width). Similarly, other elements should be sized and positioned appropriate for human height. Exploring this option, investigate the elements of each scene we currently have. Games like bridge over lava are difficult here, because scaling up the bridge might mean very little lava, meaning very little peril.

During planning, propose solutions. Explore alternative solutions, too. Are there interesting ways for players to 'get around' rooms whilst scaled down?

- Could we scale their motion / positioning, too? eg. a small step to the right moves the player a bit extra to the right etc.
- Conveyor belts between parts of the screen?
- Teleporters?
- Other mechanisms?

Extra elements (belts / teleporters / etc.) would obviously change a scene's game mechanic, so may not be a good approach here (although we may wish to implement them in future).
