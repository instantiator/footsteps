Prepare a plan to project elements onto the floor, walls and backgrounds of 2.5D scenes.

# Context

This follows [002.03.06](<002.03.06.prompt - floor placement.md>), which places items on the floor. This prompt adds surfaces: a floor composed of tiles, walls around it, and backgrounds behind them, all drawn in the floor's perspective.

# Surfaces

The engine works out each surface's four corners on screen, through the floor geometry module from [002.03.04](<002.03.04.prompt - calibration.md>), and maps the element's box onto them with a CSS `matrix3d` transform. This is native CSS with no new dependency. The element's content (text, images, anything) is drawn in perspective.

| Attribute or class                                       | Surface                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `data-floor-area="left back right front"`                | That rectangle of the floor, laid flat. Use several to compose a floor from tiles.                                                                                                                       |
| `floor-background`                                       | The whole floor, laid flat, behind any floor areas.                                                                                                                                                      |
| `rear-wall`, `left-wall`, `right-wall`, `front-wall`     | Upright on that edge of the floor, at the full length of the edge. Its height is in the same units as `data-floor-height`.                                                                               |
| `rear-background`, `left-background`, `right-background` | A backdrop behind the wall on that side: upright, standing on the ground a distance beyond the floor's edge, and stretched to reach the top and sides of the viewport. It fills any gaps in the wall.    |
| `ground-background`                                      | The ground, laid flat: the floor's plane carried on beyond its edges, to the sides and bottom of the viewport and back to the horizon. For an image that spans the whole screen, not just the play area. |
| `horizon-background`                                     | The furthest backdrop, at infinity: upright, with its bottom edge on the horizon, filling up to the top of the viewport.                                                                                 |

- **All optional.** A scene uses whichever surfaces it needs, or none. Outdoors, leave out the rear background and let the horizon show; indoors, a full-height rear wall or background hides the horizon, as a real room would.
- **One projection for every upright surface.** A background is a wall set further back and made big enough to fill the view, so walls and backgrounds share one calculation.
- **Distance.** `data-wall-distance` sets how far beyond the floor's edge an upright surface stands, in floor units: y units for the rear, x units for the sides. Walls default to `0`, on the edge. Backgrounds default to something like `50`, to be tuned when the demo is built.
- **Depth, not screen regions.** Because backgrounds stand at a distance on the ground, they shrink and move with the calibration as walls do.
- **Painting order, back to front:** `horizon-background`, `ground-background`, the rear, left and right backgrounds, `floor-background`, floor areas, then walls and floor-placed items. Transparent parts of any surface show what's behind it.
- **Walls sort among players** like floor-placed items: the rear wall is always behind them, the front wall always in front. A front wall stands between the players and the camera and hides their legs, so it only really works as a low fence. Say so in the docs.
- **Upright means upright on screen.** That assumes the camera isn't rolled; a rolled camera makes walls lean slightly.
- **The horizon** is the line through the floor's vanishing points: where its side edges meet, and where its front and back edges meet (or parallel to them, if they don't). With the default corners it is level, about 3% below the top of the viewport. It slants if the calibration isn't symmetrical, and `horizon-background` slants with it. A steeper camera, or a back edge dragged lower, can push it above the viewport, and then `horizon-background` isn't seen.
- **Feet interactions with a floor area** test the player's floor position against its rectangle, as the element's bounding box on screen is wider than the area.
- **In 2D scenes**, these attributes and classes are a validation warning, as they do nothing there.

# Real-camera checks

Add to `docs/testing/manual-testing.md`:

- Floor areas, walls and backgrounds line up with the calibrated floor, and follow it when the calibration changes.
- Standing on a floor area fires its feet interactions, and standing just off its corner doesn't.
