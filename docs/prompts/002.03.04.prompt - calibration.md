Prepare a plan to add floor calibration, the 2.5D scene mode, and fitting the view to the floor.

# Context

This follows [002.03.02](<002.03.02.prompt - scene and avatar modes.md>), which adds scene and avatar modes. It adds the `2.5D` scene mode, the floor it needs, and the camera view fitted around that floor. [002.03.06](<002.03.06.prompt - floor placement.md>) then places items on the floor.

Each 2.5D scene has a _conceptual_ floor layout. For a given game, the camera is not expected to move, so the floor layout for all scenes is the same. It's important that a player be able to walk around every part of the floor area - ie. their feet should be planted on the floor. To achieve this, 2.5D scenes are rescaled to fit around the floor area.

# Floor geometry

One module owns every conversion between camera image, viewport and floor coordinates, and nothing else in the code does this maths. The later prompts in this series use it too.

The four corners of the floor in the camera image define a projective transform (homography) between the floor and the image, and its inverse. Rows further back bunch together as they do on camera.

# The floor's shape

The floor is any convex quadrilateral: each corner can be dragged anywhere, as long as the shape stays convex. A drag that would break that stops at the limit.

This replaces the symmetrical shape first proposed, with corners that mirrored their partners. The homography is the same calculation for any convex quadrilateral, so freeing the corners costs nothing in the maths, and "drag each corner to a corner of your play space" is simpler to explain than mirrored bars. It also fits a camera that isn't centred on the play space. The consequences, all handled below:

- The front edge can slant, so the crop uses the floor's bounding box.
- Walls are upright on screen, which assumes the camera isn't rolled. A rolled camera makes them lean slightly.
- The horizon can slant (see [002.03.08](<002.03.08.prompt - floor areas walls and backgrounds.md>)).

The corners start at these positions in the camera image:

| Corners | Left | Right | From top |
| ------- | ---- | ----- | -------- |
| Front   | 8%   | 92%   | 90%      |
| Back    | 25%  | 75%   | 55%      |

These are calculated, not measured, for a 4:3 webcam with a 42° vertical field of view, 2 m high and tilted down 22°. In that setup, a back edge at 1/3 height is about 7.5 m away, and the floor would only be about a third of the image wide there, not 60%. The default back edge is about 4.5 m away, with the whole body in frame at both front and back. The front inset keeps feet and bodies away from the image edge, where tracking is weakest. The plan must record this derivation, and the user documentation should explain it so people know what the defaults assume.

# Calibration view

![The calibration view, with the default floor area, its four corners, and its buttons](<illustrations/002.03.04 - calibration view.svg>)

- **Where.** A calibrate button on the camera preview of each game's landing page opens it. The landing page's camera preview shows the floor area too, so it's visible during game setup.
- **When it's required.** At least once before a game with 2.5D scenes using the camera can be started or resumed. Enforce this on the `/play` route too, because direct links skip the landing page. Exempt games without a camera and people playing by mouse. Browser tests need a saved calibration.
- **Corners.** Each corner is a circle, with a grab cursor when the mouse is over it, joined to the others by lines. Use pointer events. For keyboard users, Tab moves between corners and the arrow keys move the focused one.
- **Players.** Highlight each player's feet, hands and head. Each foot shows green inside the floor and red outside it. Calibration expects one person: if more are in view, warn, and pause the walking check and disable Save until there is one again, while still showing everyone.
- **Instructions on screen**, in plain words, so there's nothing separate to read. For example: "Drag each blue corner to a corner of the space you can walk around in." Then: "Walk to each corner. Your feet should stay green." Then: "Press Save when it fits."
- **Buttons** labelled with what they do: "Reset to the default area" puts this camera's corners back to the defaults; "Save"; and "Forget all saved areas" clears every stored calibration, after asking for confirmation.
- **Saved state.** Show when a calibration has been saved, and on the landing page, whether this camera has one.
- **Storage.** Per camera rather than per game, because it describes the room. In image coordinates, so mirroring doesn't invalidate it.
- **Without a camera**, 2.5D scenes use the default corners. Check that the viewport's shape that results looks reasonable.

# Fitting the view to the floor

- **Crop the camera, not the scenes.** Crop to the floor's width, from the top of the image down to its lowest point, and position the camera canvases so the floor spans the viewport's width and its lowest point sits on the bottom edge. With the default corners, that is the front edge.

  ![Cropping the camera image to the floor, and the viewport that results](<illustrations/002.03.04 - crop.svg>)

- **Reshape the viewport to the crop.** Fitting the floor to the screen's width can push the back of the floor, and the heads of players standing there, off the top of the screen when the calibrated area is small. So the viewport takes the crop's shape instead, and side bars take up the slack, as they do today when the viewport reshapes to fit the camera. Scenes laid out in `cqw` and `cqh` reflow to the new shape.

  ![Why the viewport reshapes rather than scaling to the floor's width](<illustrations/002.03.04 - why reshape.svg>)

- **Interactions.** `maskRectForElement` already maps through the canvas's on-screen rectangle, so most camera interactions should keep working. Camera scenes in dialogs need checking.
- **No special case.** Use the same calculation for the default and any other calibration.
- **2D scenes aren't cropped.** They show the camera as scenes do today. A game mixing 2D and 2.5D scenes reshapes the viewport between them, which reuses the animation it already runs when the camera changes.

## Cropping what the model sees

Cropping the frame given to the vision model as well could help. Its input is resized to a fixed width, so a narrower frame either runs faster at the same scale, or shows players larger, which helps find people at the back. Drawing and feathering the cut-outs also costs less with fewer pixels.

Keep a margin around the crop, so feet at the front edge and arms reaching past the sides aren't lost; people wholly outside the crop are no longer tracked, which suits ignoring spectators later. Make this a separate, last stage of the plan, and keep it only if the vision bench (see [ADR 0005](../ADRs/0005-vision-models.md)) shows a gain.

# Debug view

With `?debug=true`:

- The floor area is drawn over every 2.5D scene.
- Players' feet, hands and head are highlighted, using the same drawing as the calibration view. This replaces the colour for every body part.

# Real-camera checks

The geometry can be unit tested, and browser tests run the camera pipeline against a fake webcam feed. Neither shows whether the floor model holds in a real room, which only a person walking in front of a real webcam can confirm. Add these to `docs/testing/manual-testing.md`:

- The default corners, and a calibration dragged to fit, line up with the real floor in the camera preview.
- Walking to every edge of the calibrated floor, the player's feet stay tracked and green, and their body stays in view at the back.
- A second person stepping in shows the warning and pauses calibration.
- A small calibration reshapes the viewport as illustrated, with nothing important cut off.
