Track the intention to add 3D scenes. This prompt is a placeholder: it isn't ready to plan yet.

# Context

[002.03.02](<002.03.02.prompt - scene and avatar modes.md>) adds scene and avatar modes, and the prompts after it build the 2D and 2.5D scene modes. This prompt adds the **3D** scene mode, where scenes are built from real 3D objects rather than HTML elements projected onto the floor.

# Intentions

- Scenes use real 3D elements.
- A player's position on the play area still comes from their feet (except when they're jumping), as in 2.5D.
- The camera's position for the play area must be worked out correctly.
- 3D objects need lighting. Start with basic ambient lighting.
- Default components that are easy to add: walls, floor tiles, and basic shapes, with configurable textures mapped onto them.

# Thinking so far

None of this has been designed or tried. It comes from working out the 2.5D approach.

- **The calibration doesn't give a camera.** The four floor corners define a 2D projective transform from the floor to the screen. A 3D camera also needs its height, tilt and field of view, which the four calibrated floor corners don't pin down. The likely options are asking for one real measurement (such as the camera's height, or the floor's width), or assuming a typical field of view. The default corners in [002.03.04](<002.03.04.prompt - calibration.md>) were derived from a 4:3 webcam with a 42° vertical field of view, 2 m high and tilted down 22°, which may be a reasonable starting assumption.
- **A second rendering path.** WebGL (for example with Three.js) would render outside the HTML templates that rules, effects and state drive today. Templates, rules and effects would need a way to reach 3D objects.
- **Interactions.** Camera interactions test body parts against elements' on-screen rectangles. 3D objects would need the same, either from their projected bounds or by raycasting.
- **Sorting players.** Players are flat silhouettes. In 2.5D they sort among items by floor depth; in 3D they would need placing in the scene at their floor depth, for example as textured planes, so objects can pass in front of and behind them.
