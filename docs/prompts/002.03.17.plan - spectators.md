## Context

Everyone the camera sees is a player, including people at the side of the room or behind the floor. They get a number, count in `game.players`, fire interactions, appear in position variables, and are drawn. With a calibrated floor (002.03.04 and .06), each person has a floor position, so someone off the floor can be told apart.

### Decisions taken

| Decision                         | Choice                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Who is a spectator               | An active player whose held floor position (`PlayerPositions`) is more than a margin outside 0–100 on x or y for longer than a hold. They become a player again after the same hold back inside. The margin defaults to 5 floor units and the hold to 1000 ms, as AppConfig knobs (`spectatorMargin` and `spectatorHoldMs`) for tuning on a real camera. Only 2.5D scenes have spectators (the `floor` prop is set). Players who are leaving, frozen or removed are already not interactive, so they are never spectators. Their state and effects carry on wherever they stand. |
| Identity (user)                  | A spectator keeps their player id and number, and gets them back on returning. If the tracker loses a spectator, the registry keeps them as a note (like a removed player's), which a person appearing near there inherits. The note lasts `SPECTATOR_NOTE_MS = 5000`; after that the number is freed.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| What spectators lose             | Interactions, position variables and the count. `frames.interactive` drops them in the loop, so `playersOf`, the monitors and `PositionPublisher` never see them. `PlayerCounts.active` excludes them. `game.players` has no spectator count (user).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Reset on return elsewhere (user) | A setting `resetOnReturn` (default off). When a spectator becomes a player again more than `RETURN_DISTANCE = 25` floor units from where they left the floor, each shown challenge whose resolved setting has it on is reset with `resetChallenge` (`GameStateUtils.ts:245`), as if from the Menu. A brand-new person walking on is not a return.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| Setting                          | `spectators?: GameSpectatorsData`, beside `scene` and `avatar` in `GameModulesData`, stage, scene and reset scene. A narrower setting overrides a broader one key by key. Several shown challenges are resolved the way `AvatarModeUtils` resolves avatars: the first shown challenge with a setting wins, then the stage's, then the game's.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| How they show                    | `show: "hidden"` (the default) or `"shown"`. `"shown"` takes `look: { opacity?, filter?, static? }`, plus optional `fade: { from, to }` in floor units outside the floor, for strength by proximity.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Strength                         | It is 0 at `from` (default: the margin) and 1 at `to`, eased with smoothstep. With no `fade`, it is 1. Opacity is lerped from 1 to `look.opacity`. `static` (a proportion) is scaled by the strength into a `StaticLook`, unless an effect's static is running, which wins. `filter` is applied to the wrapper as written, with `--spectator-strength` set on it, so authors can scale it, eg. `grayscale(calc(var(--spectator-strength) * 100%))`.                                                                                                                              |
| Debug view (user)                | It ignores spectators: they are drawn like any player, with the debug tints and no spectator look, even when the setting is `hidden`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| Less vision work (user)          | The model segments the whole frame at once, so it can't skip a person. Per spectator, the loop skips gap-filling (`PersonTracker` takes the track ids not to fill), the interaction monitors (free, as above), and, when hidden, drawing their canvas (`drawPeople` and feathering). Cropping the model's input to the floor would cut inference too, but it would lose spectators, and with them identity and the reset on return. That is deferred.                                                                                                                            |
| `data-spectator`                 | Set on a shown spectator's wrapper, so CSS and tests can find them.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |

### Stage 1: Spectators in the registry and the loop (Opus)

**Brief:**

- New `camera/Spectators.ts`, with `SpectatorWatch`:
  - `update(positions, now)` returns `{ spectators: Set<id>, distances: Map<id, number>, returned: { id, distance }[] }`;
  - it keeps each id's off/on-floor timer, and where they left the floor (their last inside position);
  - it forgets ids that the registry no longer knows.
- `PlayerRegistry`:
  - `setSpectators(ids)`;
  - `lose` keeps a spectator as a note, with a `lostAt`, which `update` deletes after `SPECTATOR_NOTE_MS`;
  - the counts exclude spectators.
- `CharacterCanvas.reportAndDraw`:
  - with a floor, after `positions.update`: `watch.update`, then `registry.setSpectators`, then filter `frames.interactive`;
  - without a floor, an empty set.
- `PersonTracker.update(frame, noFill?: ReadonlySet<trackId>)`: the registry maps spectator ids to track ids (`trackIdsOf`). The ids from the frame before are good enough.
- AppConfig: `spectatorMargin` and `spectatorHoldMs`, added to `DRAWING_SETTINGS`.

**Tests:**

- `tests/Spectators.test.ts`:
  - off the floor past the margin for the hold → a spectator;
  - a brief step over the edge → still a player;
  - walking back on → a player after the hold;
  - a jump at the front edge (held position) → still a player;
  - the distance is reported.
- `tests/PlayerRegistry.test.ts`:
  - a spectator keeps their number;
  - a spectator lost and found nearby within the note time keeps their id and number;
  - after the note time, the number is freed;
  - the counts exclude spectators.
- The tracking-loop tests with a floor: a spectator isn't in `onPlayersChange`.

**Gate:** `dev-qual/scripts/check.sh --fast` + `npx vitest run`.

### Stage 3: Reset on return elsewhere (Opus)

**Brief:**

- `resetOnReturn?: boolean` on `GameSpectatorsData`.
- The loop's `returned` list is passed to a new `onReturnElsewhere` prop (`CharacterCamera` → layout → `Game`). It fires only when `distance > RETURN_DISTANCE`.
- `Game` resets each shown challenge whose resolved setting has `resetOnReturn`, with `setGameState(s => resetChallenge(s, …))`.

**Tests:**

- `Spectators.test.ts`: a return near where they left is not reported, and a return far away is.
- A Game or rules test: a return far away resets only the challenge whose setting has it on.

**Gate:** `dev-qual/scripts/check.sh --fast` + `npx vitest run`.
