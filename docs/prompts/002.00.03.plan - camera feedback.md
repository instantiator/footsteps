## Context

In real play the camera layer flickers when BodyPix loses a person for a frame. Jumps are hard to trigger and can't be configured. Silhouette edges look pixellated. The game runs at 4–6 fps. This plan fixes all four, measuring before and after every speed change.

### Findings from exploration

- **A React re-render on every frame.** `GameLayerWrappers.tsx` passes `setFeetPositions` to `CharacterCanvas` as `onFeetPositionsChange`. The loop calls it every frame with a new object, so `GameLayersLayout` and every display layer under it re-render once per camera frame. Only the debug overlay reads `feetPositions`.
- **The loop is serial** (`startTrackingLoop` in `CharacterCanvas.tsx`). It runs detect, then tracker, then monitors, then draw, then waits for the next video frame with `requestVideoFrameCallback`, and only then detects again. The worker sits idle while the main thread does its work, so frame time is the sum of the steps rather than the larger of them.
- **The draw allocates and awaits twice per frame** (`DrawPeople.ts`). Each frame gets a new W×H×4 buffer and `await createImageBitmap(ImageData)`. The mask is binary (alpha 0 or 255), which is why the edges look jagged.
- **The monitors measure every body part for every target** (`bodyPartsIn` in `VisionQueries.ts`), even when a rule only needs `foot`. `BODY` scans the whole target rectangle. `isTopmostAt` calls `elementFromPoint`, which can force a layout.
- **The tracker centres each person by scanning their whole mask** (`centreOf` in `Tracking.ts`). Tracks expire after `TRACK_TIMEOUT_MS = 500`.
- **A dropout resets the monitors.** `usePlayerInteractions` clears every monitor when a frame has no players, and deletes the monitors of players it didn't see. A dropout during a jump therefore loses the jump.
- **The jump is a velocity state machine** (`PlayerMonitor.ts`: `JUMP_SPEED_THRESHOLD = 0.3` frame heights per second, a 1500 ms window). Velocity comes from consecutive frame pairs, and measures in frame heights, so a child far from the camera makes small numbers. A non-athletic jump is in the air for about 0.25–0.5 s, which at 5 fps is 1–3 frames, too few for velocity phases to work reliably.
- **The bench runs with `debug=true`.** The readout only shows in debug, so the bench draws the coloured mask (extra `tintPixels`), and its fps is not the fps of normal play.

### Decisions taken

| Decision     | Choice                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Flicker      | **Hold unmatched people** by default. If a tracked person goes unmatched, their last mask is still drawn (with the live video through it) for `holdMs`, then fades out over `fadeMs`, and comes back at once when they're matched again. A person who moved is matched where they now are, so they leave no trail. **Shifted per-pixel union** is an option, off by default (`cameraFillGaps`). All the approaches go in an ADR.                           |
| Held people  | Held masks are only drawn. They never fire interactions. Their monitors (and any jump in progress) survive until the track expires.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Jump model   | One detector per player, based on displacement rather than velocity. It uses the lower foot's y as a fraction of the player's body height (feet to `headTop`). The phases are grounded, then a rise of at least `minRise`, a peak, then a descent of at least `minDescent` × the rise, all within `maxAirtimeMs`. The detector notes the targets the feet were on at take-off and at landing. Frames with the feet missing are tolerated while in the air. |
| Interactions | `jump`: take-off and landing on the same element. `jump-from`: a completed jump took off from this element. `jump-onto`: a completed jump landed on this element, from anywhere. All three fire on landing.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| Latency      | Measure, then fix. The model settings stay as they are.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |

### Challenges to the prompt (for the user's review)

- **Hold length.** A 1 s hold leaves a ghost for 1 s when someone walks out of frame. A dropout at 5 fps lasts 1–2 frames (200–400 ms). The recommended defaults are a **400 ms hold and a 300 ms fade**, configurable. `TRACK_TIMEOUT_MS` becomes at least `hold + fade`, so a returning player keeps their id.
- **"Upward motion for a period".** At 5 fps the period is 0–1 frame pairs. Measuring displacement against the ground level the player stood at works at any frame rate. Sideways motion is ignored, as the prompt asks.
- **Perspective.** Stepping towards or away from the camera moves the feet in the image the way a jump does. Stepping back looks like a rise, but with no descent after it, so it isn't a jump. Stepping forward looks like a descent with no rise. Using the _lower_ foot (`feetOf` already does) means a walking step, with one foot lifted, isn't a rise.

### Researched defaults (written into `.env`; stage 8 cites the sources in the ADR)

These come from countermovement-jump data for untrained adults (about 20–35 cm) and children aged 6–10 (about 12–25 cm). Flight time is t = 2√(2h/g), which gives 0.3–0.55 s. Playful hops can be as low as about 5 cm, or 0.2 s.

| Setting                     | Default | Meaning                                                                                 |
| --------------------------- | ------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `VITE_JUMP_MIN_RISE`        | 0.04    | The rise the feet need, as a fraction of body height (about 5 cm for a 1.3 m child)     |
| `VITE_JUMP_STILL`           | 0.02    | The jitter tolerated while grounded, as a fraction of body height (foot-centroid noise) |
| `VITE_JUMP_MIN_GROUNDED_MS` | 100     | How long the feet are still before take-off counts                                      |
| `VITE_JUMP_MAX_RISE_MS`     | 500     | The longest time from take-off to peak                                                  |
| `VITE_JUMP_MAX_AIRTIME_MS`  | 1000    | The longest time from take-off to landing (flight plus a frame on each side at 5 fps)   |
| `VITE_JUMP_MIN_DESCENT`     | 0.5     | The fraction of the rise the feet must come back down                                   |
| `VITE_CAMERA_HOLD_MS`       | 400     | How long a dropped-out person is held                                                   |
| `VITE_CAMERA_FADE_MS`       | 300     | The fade after the hold                                                                 |
| `VITE_CAMERA_FILL_GAPS`     | false   | The shifted per-pixel union                                                             |
| `VITE_CAMERA_FEATHER_PX`    | 3       | The edge blur, in canvas pixels (0 turns it off)                                        |

### Stage 3: Pipeline the tracking loop (Opus)

- Restructure `startTrackingLoop` so that, as soon as frame N's detection resolves, frame N+1's `detectFrame` starts on the current video frame, and only then does the loop process frame N (tracker, monitors, draw). Exactly one request stays in flight (`VisionWorkerProxy` rejects a second), and the next start happens only after the previous request resolves. The `requestVideoFrameCallback` wait is used only when the video hasn't advanced (`video.currentTime` is unchanged). Cancelling still stops cleanly, and errors are still skipped and warned once.
- **Tests:** `tests/CharacterCanvas.test.tsx`, with a fake model that has controllable promises:

  - there is never more than one detection in flight;
  - after the loop stops, no further `detect` call is made;
  - a failed detection doesn't stop the loop.
- **Gate:** `check.sh --fast`, the unit tests, `scripts/run-browser-tests.sh camera`, and the bench in plain mode. Record the fps change.

### Stage 5: Cheaper draw and feathered edges (Opus)

### Stage 6: Hold unmatched people (Opus)

- `PersonTracker` keeps each track's last `VisionPerson`. `update` returns the frame's people, plus held people for the tracks that are unmatched but were seen within `holdMs + fadeMs`, with `opacity` set to 1 during the hold, then easing linearly to 0. Track expiry becomes `max(TRACK_TIMEOUT_MS, holdMs + fadeMs)`. Holding needs smoothing on (`visionSmoothing`); with it off nothing is held, and the docs say so.
- `PlayerFrame` gains `held?: boolean` and `opacity?: number`. `silhouettePixels` writes `255 × opacity` per person. In `usePlayerInteractions`, a held player keeps its monitor, which counts as seen but isn't updated. A frame with only held players no longer clears the monitors. Feet for the debug overlay come only from people who aren't held.
- **Tests:**
  - `tests/Tracking.test.ts`: a person missing for one frame is returned held, with opacity 1; after the hold, opacity falls; after hold plus fade they're gone. If they reappear they get the same id and aren't held. A person who moved is matched, not held, and leaves no ghost.
  - `tests/DrawPeople.test.ts`: the opacity scales the alpha.
  - `tests/PlayerInteractionHook` (in `GameLayersLayout.test.tsx`, or its own file): a held frame fires no event, and the monitor survives it.
- **Gate:** `check.sh --fast`, the unit tests, and the camera browser suite, where a fake camera that blanks one frame shows no flicker (the canvas alpha stays above 0).

### Stage 7: Shifted per-pixel union, off by default (Opus)

- With `cameraFillGaps` on, each matched person's mask becomes `current OR previous`, with the previous mask shifted by the centre's movement in whole pixels. This happens for one frame only, in the tracker, using the centres it already computes. The result is used for drawing only, not for monitors, so a limb that is filled in can't trigger anything.
- **Tests:** `tests/Tracking.test.ts`: with fill on, a person whose legs disappear for a frame keeps their shifted legs, and a second missing frame drops them. With fill off, the masks are unchanged.
- **Gate:** `check.sh --fast`, the unit tests, and the bench with `&cameraFillGaps=true`. Record the draw cost.

### Stage 8: Jump detector, `jump-from` and `jump-onto` (Opus)

- Add `src/components/camera/JumpDetector.ts`, a pure class per player. Its `update(feetY | null, bodyHeight, timestamp)` returns `"takeoff" | "landed" | undefined`, using the settings in the defaults table:
  - The **grounded** state follows the ground level. When the feet go lower in the image, or move within `still × h` of the ground, the ground moves to them.
  - Rising more than `minRise × h` above the ground, after at least `minGroundedMs` grounded, is a **takeoff**.
  - **Airborne** tracks the peak. It is abandoned if the peak is still rising after `maxRiseMs`, or if more than `maxAirtimeMs` passes. When abandoned, the ground resets to the current feet.
  - A descent of at least `minDescent × (ground − peak)` is a **landing**.
  - A null `feetY` while airborne is skipped.
  - Body height is the feet's y minus the `headTop` y. With no head, it is 0.5 × the frame height.
- `PlayerMonitor` owns one detector. Each frame it notes the jump targets the feet cover (`isCovered("foot", …)`), and keeps the set from the last grounded frame. On `landed`, it fires `jump` for the keys in both the take-off and landing sets, `jump-from` for the take-off keys, and `jump-onto` for the landing keys. Delete `JUMP_SPEED_THRESHOLD`, `JumpPhase`, `nextJumpPhase`, `jumpExpired` and the per-target `jumps` map.
- Add `"jump-from" | "jump-onto"` to `GameInteraction` (`GameDisplayData.ts`), `isCameraInteraction`, and anywhere else `"jump"` is listed (grep for it). Then regenerate the schemas and TSDoc.
- The jump settings join `AppConfig` and `.env`, with comments, and reach `PlayerMonitor` through `usePlayerInteractions`.
- **Tests:** `tests/JumpDetector.test.ts` drives synthetic feet sequences at 5 and 30 fps:
  - a jump on the spot lands;
  - a jump forward (landing lower in the image) lands;
  - a jump back (landing higher, still with ≥ 50% descent) lands;
  - stepping back (a rise with no descent) does nothing, and the ground resets;
  - walking forward does nothing;
  - jitter within `still` does nothing;
  - a one-frame dropout mid-air still lands;
  - a rise slower than `maxRiseMs` is abandoned;
  - a child-sized body height behaves the same as an adult's.

  `tests/PlayerMonitor.test.ts` has a jump from A to B (`jump-from` A, `jump-onto` B, no `jump`), a jump on A (all three on A), and no events from held frames. The old velocity tests are replaced.

- **Gate:** `check.sh --fast`, the unit tests, `generate-schemas.sh` with a clean diff afterwards, and the camera browser suite.

## Baseline

Measured at stage 1 on the development Mac (headless Chromium, WebGPU, fake 1280×720 camera at 30 fps). This machine is faster than the user's, which plays at 4–6 fps.

| Bench                                                | fps  | inference ms | track ms | monitor ms | draw ms | React commits/s |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ---- | ------------ | -------- | ---------- | ------- | --------------- |
| Photos, plain (`VISION_BENCH_PLAIN=1`), median of 26 | 10.2 | 67.3         | 0.8      | 0.1        | 2.4     | —               |
| Balloons challenge, one person                       | 15.1 | 42.7         | 0.5      | 0.2        | 2.0     | 16.1            |

- 15.1 fps is exactly every second frame of a 30 fps camera. Detect (43 ms) plus processing comes to just over one frame (33 ms), and the serial loop then waits for the next video frame. The pipelining in stage 3 targets that ceiling.
- Track, monitor and draw are small (under 5 ms even with 7–8 people). Stage 4 is skipped by its own condition, as `monitor-ms` is under 1% of frame time.
- React commits once per camera frame, from `setFeetPositions`. Stage 2 removes that.
- **After stage 2:** the balloons bench gives 14.6 fps (within noise of the baseline, as this machine isn't main-thread bound) and **2.7 React commits/s, down from 16.1**.
